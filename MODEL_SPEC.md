# Possessions v9 — 2026-11-14

This model uses ESPN rosters, injury designations and completed box scores. Box-score requests are restricted to event IDs in the permitted 2026/2027 public outcome history, at most 400 requested events per refresh. Recent completed boxes are revisited for corrections. The aggregate legacy shooting cache is not used for fitting; its population could not be audited by event. All production shooting and counting inputs now come from the same validated regular-season boxes.

This is an uncalibrated simulator. No sportsbook prices, spread captions or market totals enter fitting or sampling. Market comparisons are validation observations. A result near a posted line does not establish forecast accuracy.

## Data and publication

Required scoreboard, outcome, roster and injury failures stop publication. A malformed injury response is not an empty injury report. Explicitly empty reports are accepted. Completed boxes must contain both opponents, valid nonnegative integer counts, shooting identities, reconciled scores and plausible complete minutes. Partial/live/invalid boxes stay retryable. Regular-season fitting excludes preseason, postseason and play-in events. The 2026/2027 season restriction remains intentional until the permitted history lifecycle is expanded.

`public/data/matchups.json` is optional and is not in the manifest. It is a descriptive report from the shooting cache and the committed slate and rate card. Every game's verdict is NO BET. It does not feed fitting, sampling, or the picks record.

The slate and card have matching generation timestamps and SHA-256 checksums in a shared manifest. Both are validated before either public file is replaced; clients retry up to three coherent sets when generations or hashes differ. Unsupported model versions fail closed. A GitHub Pages artifact publishes both together. Publication requires usable active rosters, finite rates, per-team shooting exposure and unique athlete memberships. The browser rejects mismatched generations, wrong Chicago dates and source checks older than six hours (90 minutes within four hours of a tip), and rechecks after returning to the tab and every minute. These are operational expiry rules, not guarantees about injury-feed latency.

The off-site shooting cache stores completed event records so aggregates can be recomputed after corrections. `model_version`, `generator_sha`, `source_cutoff` and an input SHA-256 identify a card. Pre-tip cards and slates are archived under `data/forecast-archive/`; they and the shooting cache never enter `dist/`. Source bytes have a separate model hash. Identical substantive inputs and source hashes retain the card timestamp, bytes and seed. Successful source checks are recorded separately in ignored `.refresh-status.json` and passed to deployment as a matching health attestation; health is added only to the deployed artifact. Local rate-card builds require an explicit GENERATOR_SHA (a 40-character source commit or the deliberate scratch marker pending). Missing provenance and local-uncommitted are rejected, including when reusing an earlier generation. A pending card is explicitly uncommitted; it does not claim a committed source revision. Historical roster/injury snapshots were not retroactively invented.

## Minutes and participation

Long-term rate exposures weight the prior season 0.2 and current season 1. Current-role minutes are fitted separately: chronologically selected 5/10-appearance windows and optional 2/5-appearance half-lives blend with observed prior-season minutes (or the fitted population prior if absent). The prior strength is selected from 0/1/3/6/10 games by chronological minute-error comparison on preceding-season appearances. A separate final block measures minute errors and starter/bench residual dispersion. Fewer than 100 validation appearances retain a labelled three-game prior. Starts, team changes and evidence ages are recorded. Injury-return-specific effects remain unknown.

New summaries retain explicit participant/DNP records. Participation follows each team's explicit boxes: a player listed by a team (played or DNP) is tracked there until another team lists him, and a later explicit team box without him is an absence. Legacy boxes without explicit participation add nothing; omission there stays unknown. A rostered player never listed is absent for that team's explicit boxes this season; a player last listed elsewhere keeps that stint and is absent for the new team's later boxes. Features are role minutes over the last ten played games, played share of the last five team games, played last game, start share, absent share of the last five and the missed-game streak. The logistic fit uses Newton steps with ridge penalties 0.1/1/10/100 chosen on a chronological 60/20/20 event split, and is used only when the untouched block beats the base rate in both log loss and Brier. Training skips the first missed game after a played game: that is usually an injury or rest day that the pre-tip report already lists. Archived pre-tip reports then calibrate players listed healthy with a two-parameter logistic map on the model logit, fitted on the earlier 70% of archived games and used only when it improves the later 30% (at least 300 rows). Uncertain designations multiply that baseline by the archived share of same-designation players who played (at least 30 rows); otherwise labelled, uncalibrated multipliers apply: probable 0.9, doubtful 0.1, questionable/day-to-day 0.5. A designation-level rate never replaces a healthy player's own history. Out/suspended/inactive players receive zero. A player with no history at all keeps the Beta(1,1) scenario prior with known/unknown coverage counts. A rostered player with no NBA appearance in the permitted history starts from newcomer minutes: the mean of the first three appearances by players without a preceding-season appearance (a labelled ten-minute prior until 20 such appearances exist). Reported minute restrictions have a separate field and full-game cap; no restriction is inferred from an injury description. Historical injury-calibration fitting requires settled, pre-tip roster observations; without those records, status probabilities remain priors. Joint availability is sampled conditional on at least five players capable of full regulation and overtime minutes. This conditioning can raise marginal participation rates. The UI reports the actual simulated play share separately.

Positive minutes targets receive a mean-preserving gamma multiplier with the fitted residual SD (coefficient of variation capped at one) and proportional capped allocation to 240 team minutes; a truncated normal would add minutes to low-minute players before the budget is rescaled. There is no 24-minute cutoff or sequential bench residual assignment. Overtime happens only after a tie, uses the same participants and caps every player's increment at five, totaling 25 per team. Integer minute allocations are realized as one-minute stints with exactly five distinct players on each side. Every attempt, rebound, steal, block and assist is assigned only to a player in that stint. Observed starts and closing stints guide feasible synthetic rotations. Score/time strata can change starter/bench selection only if their fitted probabilities improve a later event block over the time-only baseline. Personal-foul hazards are estimated from box-reconciled play histories and fractional on-court exposure, including fouls at substitution boundaries. Frozen hazards generate Poisson increments at minute boundaries; conditioning on previous foul count is enabled only after improving held-out point-process loss. A sixth foul removes subsequent stints and overtime eligibility. If the sampled rotation is exhausted, a healthy coach-DNP with sufficient remaining minute capacity can enter; remaining available (including questionable) roster players and labelled replacement-level league-average lines can fill after that, including during a draw when a short-handed team that started with eight or nine healthy players still drops below five eligible after foul-outs. Replacement-level lines copy observed rates from the card; they are not invented player stats. They are added only when the live rotation cannot field five, so ordinary draws of that game are unchanged. A team with no listed players, or with no observed shooting rates to copy, still fails visibly. Thin rotations stay playable and are flagged on the slate and box score. This is a minute-resolution approximation, not a positional depth chart or a validated joint foul/FT process. Participation is drawn before tip and does not depend on the score. Deep-bench players appear mostly in blowouts and then play a few garbage-time minutes (on 61 archived games, players with under eight role minutes appeared in 17% of games decided by 12 or fewer and 68% of larger margins), while the sampler gives them their average minutes in any game and the score/time strata add garbage time on top; their minutes when they appear run about 25% high. A score-dependent entry model for players outside the planned rotation is not fitted yet.

Version 9 changes four things. The first three were each measured on 103 archived pre-tip games replayed with the source. First, the healthy calibration gains a monotone step after the logistic map: pool-adjacent-violators over 60-row blocks of the calibrated probability, fitted on the earlier 70% of archived healthy rows and kept only when it lowers log loss on the later 30%. A logistic map cannot bend twice, and it left healthy players carded for 25 minutes or more at 95% to 97% when they played 99% or more of the time, and fringe players too low; participation log loss fell from 0.307 to 0.300. Second, regulation minutes no longer use one gamma shape. `minutes_spread` holds 21 quantile knots of actual over predicted minutes for each of eight predicted-minutes tiers, scored on appearances the role predictor had not seen (the final fifth of preceding-season games and every active-season game before the cutoff). Each tier is scaled to average one, so only the shape is fitted: narrow and capped for starters, a long low tail for reserves. One uniform draw per player replaces the gamma multiplier; overtime periods and older cards keep the gamma. Proportional rescaling to 240 still adds a shared factor with a standard deviation near 0.09, so a 30-to-34-minute player's simulated minutes remain wider than observed (0.22 against 0.17 of his role minutes). Third, per-minute counting rates shrink toward players in the same minutes-per-game tier (under 12, 12 to 20, 20 to 28, 28 and over) and position, scaled by the team's level relative to the league, once that pool holds 2,000 minutes; the team/position blend remains the fallback. A reserve was being pulled toward a team average that is mostly starters' minutes: players carded for 5 to 10 minutes were simulated at 0.414 points a minute against 0.368 scored, and 0.392 after the change; for 10 to 15 minutes, 0.401 against 0.385, and 0.388 after. Reserves' minutes when they play still run about one minute high. Fourth, a short-handed rotation is no longer rescaled to 240 as it stands. A player's expected regulation minutes are held to twelve more than his role minutes or 1.6 times them, whichever is larger; the rest share what is left, and the limits relax in proportion only when every player is at his own. When the drawn rotation cannot cover 240 minutes at those limits (hard caps included), the remaining healthy players enter by role minutes, as they already did when foul-outs emptied a rotation; a player who missed his draw on an uncertain designation still sits. Before this, a team with seven players ruled out put three 20-to-25-minute reserves at 42 to 44 simulated minutes each, with healthy reserves left out at an 8% play probability; it now plays all ten available. Hard caps and the noise draw are unchanged, so a rotation whose roles all scale inside their limits is drawn exactly as before: 138 of the 150 pinned seeded draws are byte-identical, and the 12 that changed are short-handed.

## Possessions and production

The box-score possession estimator is `FGA + 0.44 FTA - ORB + TOV`. The 0.44 coefficient is the conventional approximation; FT trip sizes and types are estimated from numbered play sequences that reconcile with player box attempts. A game's expected pace averages the two fitted team paces; its standard deviation comes from historical regulation-equivalent pace. Each draw gives both teams the same integer possession budget. Overtime uses the same pace scaled to five minutes.

Team turnovers, offensive rebounds and assists are shrunk toward the observed league population with a 12-game prior; team pace uses a six-game prior, which lowered walk-forward total error. Per-minute counting rates (attempts, free throws, rebounds, assists, steals, blocks, turnovers) shrink toward the team/position mean with a 75-minute prior and count last season at half weight; on 13,171 out-of-sample player-games the earlier 200-minute prior with a 0.2 weight pulled high-volume players down and role players up. Active players divide shot/FT opportunities by minutes times fitted attempt rates raised to a fitted concentration exponent: in reconstructed lineup stints a shot, free-throw trip or assist goes to higher-usage players more than proportionally (about 1.15 for shots and trips, 1.1 for assists; rebounds stay proportional). Each build refits the exponents on the earlier 70% of stints and keeps them only where they beat proportional splitting on the later 30%. A player's per-minute rate is a share of team events across the lineups that player actually plays in, so a unit's assists, offensive rebounds and free-throw trips scale with its players' summed (concentrated) rates, divided by the minute average of that sum over the game's planned stints so team totals stay with the team model. Assists also use a per-teammate-make propensity: the assist rate divided by one minus the player's share of the unit's makes (capped at 0.6), because the rate already counts only teammates' baskets and the shooter is skipped on each make. A make is assisted with the team assist rate times the teammates' summed propensities over that planned average; averaged over shooters this equals the unit's summed rates, so each player's expected assists follow the rate. Offensive-rebound odds scale with the offense unit's summed rebound rates over the defense unit's, each relative to its planned average. Splitting a fixed number of events each minute instead gave stars, who play beside other high-rate starters, less than their rates and bench units more; on 61 archived pre-tip games the lead playmaker's simulated assists moved from 14% to 1% below the card rate (15% to 3% below actual), the lead free-throw drawer's attempts from 4% below to 2% above the card rate, and team margins and totals did not shift beyond Monte Carlo noise. Box scores credit about 85% of non-offensive-rebound misses to a defender; the rest are team rebounds with no player credit, and the sampler now leaves the same share uncredited. On the archived pre-tip games, the team's top two projected scorers moved from 11.6% to 5.7% below their actual points, player rebounds from 21% to 7% high, and player points error fell from 3.96 to 3.88. Team target efficiency is the fitted team offense times a personnel factor (tonight's lineup implied PPP over the lineup the team actually played: each player's average minutes per team game this season, zeros included, so a season-long absence is not charged twice; older cards fall back to role minutes), multiplied by opponent defensive efficiency relative to the league and a venue term. The personnel factor is clamped to [0.7, 1.25]. Full-strength lineups keep the team rating; absences scale it. There is no geometric-mean shrinkage of team offense toward league-like player rates. Make probabilities are solved against this target before shots are sampled; scores are never adjusted after drawing. Offense, opponent defense, venue and log-rest differences are fitted together with ridge shrinkage. Earlier seasons get their own scoring-level shift, so the intercept is this season's league efficiency and also normalizes targets; a pooled intercept kept last season's higher scoring. Games are weighted by recency with a 90-day half-life on top of the season weight; on an 826-game walk-forward (January 2026 through November 2026) that beat no decay and 45-, 120- and 150-day half-lives on margin and total error, while letting the internal holdout choose the half-life overreacted. Team, prior-season and level-shift strengths are selected by chronological holdout of predicted points (penalties 4/8/12, prior-season weights 0.2/0.5/1, level-shift penalties 50/200/800) and then refit on all events; venue/rest still use a 50-observation zero-effect prior. Neutral games omit the venue term. Rest is derived from Chicago calendar dates, including DST, with prior overtime workload. Travel distance and timezone changes require coordinates/timezones in permitted source responses and otherwise remain null. These are observational estimates, not causal effects.

Each possession can end in a turnover, field goal or ordinary FT trip. Numbered FT sequences fit two/three-shot shooting and bonus trip mixes, and-one probability, technical rate and retained-possession penalties. Unclassified trips stay labelled other. Personal take fouls are not automatically treated as retained-possession transition take fouls. Only field-goal misses and missed final ordinary/and-one free throws create rebound opportunities. Technical and retained-possession trips never create a rebound or consume an ordinary possession. A late leading-team FT adjustment (last two minutes, lead 1–8) is enabled only after improving held-out opportunity log loss. These fixed state boundaries are declared scenarios, not optimized thresholds or claims about a player's intent.

Offensive rebounds extend the possession. Offensive and defensive rebounds compete for the same opportunities; player shares use historical rebounding rates within the on-court five. Made field goals have at most one assister, excluding the scorer. Steals are a subset of opponent turnovers and blocks a subset of missed field goals. Team/dead-ball rebounds and the joint live bonus/foul/FT process remain approximations.

Lineup offense/defense uses observed substitution stints. Coefficients train on the first 60% of events, the next 20% selects the ridge penalty, and the final 20% evaluates it. Both stint and full-game baseline improvements and at least 20 completely reconstructed test games are required to enable it. The current fit fails this gate and is disabled. Creator-off-court usage also remains disabled when its untouched event block does not beat baseline; off-court associations are not causal injury effects.

Shot-mix and shooting priors use leave-athlete-out role pools and preceding-season chronological strength selection, with a separate final block for per-stat promotion. Shooting probability uncertainty uses beta posterior draws for accepted fits. Participation similarly separates training, penalty selection and final evaluation. Unknown historical roster and injury status remain explicit.

Frozen team-fit diagnostics report held-out score/efficiency covariance, margin/total SD and home/away/rest residual bias. These residuals include intrinsic possession noise, so they are not added wholesale as an extra browser shock.

Real games give back part of a lead. In permitted play histories a quarter returns a share of the lead's departure from its expected path (an out-of-sample running points rating supplies the expected margin): about 7% in the first three quarters and 11% in the fourth. Independent possessions return none, so simulated margins spread with SD near 17 points while this model's walk-forward residual SD is about 15. Each build fits early and fourth-quarter give-back on quarter-end margins from reconciled histories, keeps it only if it improves the later 30% of games, and converts it into a small tilt on field-goal makes against the team that is ahead of its expected path (fourth quarter and overtime use the late rate). The tilt acts on departures, so average margins are unchanged; simulated margin spread falls to about 15 points and simulated quarter give-back matches the real quarters. Status probabilities and joint foul/FT outcomes remain uncalibrated. Older cards without matchup context use the fallback independent-stat sampler and should not be treated as the current model.

## Evaluation and UI

`npm run sanity` replays each current matchup with 10,000 draws and `fnv1a(event_id + ':' + generated_at)`. It reports a home spread with negative meaning home favored. It does not load market lines. Computation runs in a cancellable browser worker and is cached by event, substantive input hash, model hash and the viewer's lineup calls, with generation/version fallback for old cards. Lineups lets a viewer rule any rostered player in (plays every draw at role minutes, any reported restriction still applies) or out (zero minutes) for one game. Calls are stored only in that browser, applied to a copy of the card before sampling with the same seed, labelled on the score view, and never change published files. Worker progress is reported every 250 draws.  The score view shows the 10,000-game forecast only: expected points, win shares, key numbers, margin and total distributions, and each player's average with a 10th to 90th percentile range. A single high-total draw and per-draw percentile colors were removed; one draw is mostly noise, and the distributions and player ranges already show the tails. Forecast rows display all-draw expectations, including DNPs, so minutes still sum to the team budget. Most-points, most-rebounds, and most-assists in a game are read from those same joint draws over every player on both teams who played that draw. The chance a player leads is the share of draws in which his counting stat is strictly highest; ties are dead heats, not wins. Comparing the two highest projected averages is not this market. Rankings use that dead-heat-aware lead chance, not L5/L10 hit rates or simulation means.

The owner asked that matchup and prop work verify the date, matchup, roster, and availability against the injury report on the card, then follow matchup, opportunity, historical validation, injury adjustment, probability, price, and a BET/LEAN/PASS decision. Zero bets is an allowed output. Without a posted most-in-game price the page does not invent one, and the decision is PASS. Injury tags on the projections page carry the card's generation timestamp; a listing's own report time is shown when the feed stamped it. Missing injury tags, lines, or projections stay missing. Player-panel expected lines average only draws with minutes greater than 0. Prop probabilities condition on participation and exclude pushes; the panel explains the difference. Zero-minute DNP rows never enter shooting or counting-rate samples. Steals, blocks, turnovers and milestones are unavailable on older cards without those samples. P+A, P+R, R+A and double/triple-doubles preserve draw correlations. Milestones use the five standard categories at ten or more and settle yes as over 0.5; DNPs void. Total and home-margin distributions show means, medians, intervals, mean Monte Carlo SE and entered-line probabilities including pushes.

`npm run walk-forward` refits the team model before each date since January 2026 and scores its pace-and-efficiency predictions on that date's games (no simulation, about a minute). `npm run replay` rebuilds each archived pre-tip card from its stored roster, injury report and training events with the current source, then scores 2,000 draws per game against settled boxes and the last archived pre-tip market quote (margin/total MAE and bias, win Brier, participation log loss, minutes and points errors). It is a diagnostic on a few dozen games, not a calibration certificate. `npm run backtest` writes `data/backtest-report.json`, using only event history from earlier Chicago dates. Same-date games are conservatively excluded because historical final timestamps are unavailable. It reports win Brier/log loss/reliability; margin/total CRPS, MAE, bias and interval coverage; and player-stat errors. A simple empirical historical baseline is scored on the same games, with paired game-cluster bootstrap intervals. Historical candidates are inferred from the preceding five team boxes, with unknown injuries. Ambiguous memberships are skipped and reported. Where a matching versioned, pre-tip archive with valid lineage exists, the harness replays it; otherwise the reconstruction is a diagnostic, not an archived forecast. Explicit DNP records support participation calibration where available; missing DNP/roster coverage remains unknown. Player errors cover matched actual participants, not a complete DNP/prop calibration population.

No confirmed historical closing-market archive is available. Permitted scoreboard quotes are now archived prospectively in `data/market-archive/`, independently of model inputs. Pre-tip paired moneylines support no-vig comparisons; an ESPN field named `close` is not treated as proof of an actual closing quote. Release gates require at least 500 untouched games and 2,000 settled player markets; absolute margin/total bias at most 1/2 points; 80%/90% coverage within three percentage points; ECE at most .03; and game-clustered evidence of improvement over baselines without material market-relative regression. Those gates have not passed. Prop chances are mapped by the prop calibration below, but the prop policy that allows a BET verdict remains uncalibrated. New cards publish per-stat exposure, age, prior fraction and empirical standard error; prices cannot override poor minute/role evidence. A versioned calibration artifact with as-of provenance and all release gates is required for promotion; a manually toggled boolean cannot certify a model.

The October 27 audit reports describe their named historical snapshots. Their parameters and sample forecasts are not the current specification.

## Posted lines, leans and the graded record

The picks step reads posted over/under lines and prices from PickLedger's public NBA prop price files after the card is built. A lean is the side at a posted line whose model chance (share of the draws the player played, pushes excluded) exceeds that side's break-even by more; the difference is the claimed edge. Prices never enter fitting, the card or sampling. The feed keeps a row it no longer sees, with its old stamp, so only rows from a day's last capture before a game's tip count as posted. A market the book pulled after injury news otherwise still looks priced, and a card that has the news shows a 45-point edge against a price nobody can bet.

On 9,004 settled props from 103 replayed games the posted no-vig price predicted results better than the model (Brier 0.2465 against 0.253; a coin is 0.25). Posted overs won 46% of the time. A walk-forward fit of the outcome on the price and the model, refitted before each date, put almost no weight on the model for points and did not produce a policy that beat the posted prices. A model percentage at a posted line is therefore a claim, not a pick.

So every comparison is logged and graded. `data/pick-ledger/<date>.json` records each posted line when it is first compared (the opening comparison, never rewritten), the latest comparison made before its tip (`last`: a later card, or the same card against a newer price), the last posted price before the tip, and the final box-score value; a player who sits voids the row. The record grades the latest comparison, so a live day is measured on the forecast the list showed last and a replayed day on the last card before the tip. The record groups graded leans by side and claimed edge (below 0, 0 to 3, 3 to 6, 6 to 10, 10 to 15, 15 to 20, 20 to 25, 25 and over, in probability points) and reports the hit rate, the average break-even needed, and units at the posted prices. The measured edge pools neighbouring groups until it never falls as the claim grows, then shrinks each pool toward its side with a 200-lean prior. A claim of 25 points or more is a reliability problem (the same threshold the prop verdict uses): it is graded in its own group, kept out of the pooling and the side's prior, and never listed. A lean is listed only when its group holds 200 graded leans and a measured edge of at least one point; the page shows the claim and the measured number together, and the prop verdict passes when the measured number is negative. That 200-lean and one-point gate is the live table, the same buckets measured on leans logged before the tip. Replayed days stay in the settled totals and are not what the list reads. With 5,766 graded leans from 105 games through November 13, under leans claiming 0 to 25 points measured 1.5 to 5.3 points above the price, every group of over leans measured below it, and the 29 under leans claiming 25 points or more won 34%.

Days marked `replay` were rebuilt from archived pre-tip inputs with the current source and the lines from the feed's last capture before that card was built; the v9 changes were chosen on those same games, so that part of the record is not out of sample for them. Days marked `live` were logged before the tip. The feed keeps one stamp per line, so an unchanged price cannot be told apart from an unseen one in replay, and the last posted price before a tip is not a confirmed closing line. Release gates above still apply: the record is evidence, not a calibration certificate.

## Prop calibration

The simulated over share is overconfident: on the live ledger through November 17, raw chances near 26% and 73% settled at 43% and 58%. `npm run calibrate-props` runs on every refresh before the picks step and fits one map for each stat group (points with PRA, P+A and P+R; rebounds, assists and R+A; threes; steals and blocks) from settled live rows only. Replay days are never fitted. A row is a player's posted line, scored on the chance at the latest pre-tip comparison, with voids, pushes and players under a 50% play share left out.

Maps are keyed to the card's simulation family (`model_version`, now `possessions-v9`), not to `model_hash`. The hash covers every source file, so a page or feed change would otherwise retire a map that the change did not touch. Each logged snapshot records the family that made its chance (`v`). Snapshots logged before that field existed were all made by possessions-v9, which went live on November 14 before live logging began. Bumping `MODEL_VERSION` retires every map, and only rows of the new family are fitted afterwards.

Validation is walk-forward. Each settled tip date is scored by temperature, Platt, beta and (with 1,000 fit rows) isotonic maps fitted only on the days before it, once those days hold 300 rows. The scores are pooled over the last ten such days, and the scored days need 100 player-games. The method with the lowest pooled log loss is chosen, with ties going to the simpler map. Isotonic must win two different evaluations in a row, and a map that falls as the raw chance rises is never kept. The shipped map is the chosen method fitted on every day before the last settled one, so it too was scored on a day it never saw. One evaluation over two or three days was too noisy: the points model ran against results on November 16 and 17 and with them on November 18 and 19.

A map is applied while its pooled walk-forward score beats the raw simulation on log loss, Brier score and 10-bin equal-width ECE, and while blending it with the market costs no more than 0.005 log loss against the no-vig price. Otherwise the group shows the raw simulation. The fixed 0.03 ECE bar, the Cox slope and intercept intervals, beating the no-vig price on Brier, and 500 scored rows are release checks for a BET verdict. They do not withhold a map, because withholding it shows the raw chance, which scores worse. On a few hundred lines near even, sampling noise alone puts equal-frequency ECE near 0.05, and the no-vig price itself fails a 0.03 bar there. Drift compares ECE with the 95th and 99th percentiles that a calibrated forecaster would show on the same chances. Red drift keeps the map but stops every lean in its group from being listed. A map whose last scored day is more than seven days old is not applied.

The page's model chance is then `w × calibrated + (1 − w) × no-vig`. `w` is the weight up to 0.25 with the lowest pooled log loss, and the 0.2 prior stands when those weights score within 0.002 of each other. A weight above 0.25 needs 2,000 fit rows in a group and an earlier held-out block that chose the same weight. The panel and each lean show the raw share beside the calibrated one. The ledger keeps raw chances, so each refit maps raw chances to outcomes. Mean-bias correction is not fitted: the ledger stores no projection. Each run writes `data/prop-calibration/<group>.json`, a dated report, and the published `public/data/prop-calibration.json` (the picks step loads only maps that pass).

## NBA bets of the day

The Games and Projections tabs open with the NBA bets of the day: player props and game spreads, posted one at a time per category, with a record over every posted pick. `npm run daily` (`scripts/build-daily.ts`) runs in the refresh job after the picks step. When a category has no open pick, it draws each game still to tip with the seed and draw count the page uses and posts the best offer whose price was checked within the last six hours, the longest a card stays current. (The prop feed is checked only a few times a day, so a tighter window would hide most prices.) A posted pick is fixed at its line and price and stays open until its game tips; the next run after that posts the best offer from the games still to play. A game is posted at most once per category per day. Every posted pick is kept in `data/daily-ledger/<date>.json`, shown on the day's list with its state (open, in progress, awaiting its final, or its result), and graded into the record at its posted line and price, one unit each. A spread settles on the final margin from the ESPN scoreboard, or the cached box score; a prop settles on the player's box score, and voids if he does not play. A pick whose game has no final three days after its date voids. `public/data/daily.json` holds the slate's two days of picks and the record.

A prop is a lean at its best current price. It needs a current calibration map for its stat group, validated before the tip date and not red on drift; a claimed edge of at least 3 percentage points and short of the extreme bar; and a played share of at least one half. The calibrated quote then goes through the BET/LEAN/PASS decision, with pushes and sit-outs counted, at the best price any current book gives that player, stat, line and side. A lean whose side and strength have beaten the posted price by a point across 200 live-graded leans is listed and keeps its BET or LEAN; a lean the release calibration has not certified can stay a LEAN, and a BET still needs released calibration. Any other lean that passes is an unproven edge (`EDGE`): it is graded on price and data alone, the live record of its side and strength is printed beside it, and it never outranks a BET or LEAN. The calibration maps blend the simulation with the no-vig price (weight about 0.2), so a calibrated claim of 3 points needs the simulation far from the price. On the November 24 card, the best claim across that night's 214 compared lines was 0.4 points, so on most days no prop clears the checks.

A spread reads the ESPN scoreboard's current point spread. The rate-card job attaches it to each pregame game in the published `slate.json` (`spread_quotes`: book, home handicap, both prices, capture time) after fitting and hashing, so quotes never enter fitting, the input hash, or sampling. A side's chance is its share of draws covering the handicap, pushes excluded, and the expected return counts a push as a refund. A spread needs an edge of 3 percentage points over the break-even, outside 1.96 Monte Carlo standard errors; an expected return of at least 1%; an edge under 25 points; and 200 decided draws. Spread leans have no graded record, so until the simulated margins pass their release gates a spread is an unproven edge. After that it is a LEAN, or a BET with an edge of 8 points and a return of 5%. A large gap from the posted spread is more often model error than value.

Picks rank BET, then LEAN, then EDGE, then by expected return and model chance. When a category has no pick today, the panel says why: there is no current price, or no offer has cleared the checks. When its pick has tipped and games remain, the panel says the next one posts at the next update. The record starts on November 25, with that day's four spreads rebuilt from the published artifacts by the same posting rule.

## NFL projections

`scripts/build-nfl-slate.ts` caches every finished box score of the season from ESPN game summaries in `data/nfl-cache.json` (off the site) and simulates each game on the page 10,000 times. Team points come from offense and defense ratings fitted by ridge regression on points scored (penalty 2, games weighted by a 112-day half-life), played out drive by drive: both teams share a pace, and each drive ends in a touchdown, a field goal or nothing at rates that reproduce the team's expected points and touchdown share; defensive scores and safeties are added at league rates. Pass and rush attempts follow the team's recent volume and move with the drawn margin (a team behind throws more). Targets, carries, catches, yards and touchdowns are split by each player's recency-weighted share over the team's last eight games, pulled toward small priors, so the quarterback's line is the sum of his receivers'. ESPN rosters set positions; the game summary's injury report sits players listed out or doubtful, and a questionable player plays three draws in four. A finished game is projected from the players who took part, because today's roster can be missing someone hurt in it.

Calibration is walk-forward: each game from week 4 on is projected only from the games before it, with the players who recorded a line as its active list (past injury reports are not stored). That check sets the scoring noise, a win-chance temperature, a mean-bias scale per position and stat (pulled toward 1 with 200 rows of prior), a range scale per stat so 80% of results land inside the published 10th-to-90th range, and a logistic map for the anytime-touchdown chance. Through week 11 the check covered 103 games: win Brier 0.213 (0.181 on the latest 46, where the score-only Elo scored 0.197), margin off by 9.4 points on average, and every yards range holding about 80% of results. The page prints the current check. With fewer than 60 box scores, or a check that does not beat a coin, the page falls back to the Elo. Each game publishes its two team plans and the calibration, and the card's Simulate button replays them on the page with the build's seed and draw count, so the box score it opens matches the card. A box-score row is the player's average over all 10,000 games, sits included, with his calibrated 10th-to-90th range in the games he plays.

## Esports projections

Each player's projection is his own rate from earlier games, newest weighted most (half weight every eight series back) and pulled toward the field's rate: CS2 kills and headshots per round (120 rounds of prior), Dota 2 and League of Legends kills and assists per game (3 games of prior). A CS2 projection covers maps 1 and 2, each drawn at 21.5 rounds (SD 4.2, at least 13). Counts are gamma-Poisson. Every earlier game of every loaded player is predicted from that player's games before it, which fits a mean-bias scale and the smallest spread that puts 80% of results inside the 10th-to-90th range; the page prints how many games each title was checked on and the share inside. The displayed game is never part of its own projection. No match winner or posted line is used.

## Published simulations

The deploy job draws every playable game of the built card (`scripts/publish-sims.ts`, about a minute for a full slate) with the seed and draw count the page uses, and publishes each game as `data/sims/<event>.json` beside an index. A file holds the summary the box score shows (scores, means and ranges, win shares, distributions, the shown draw) and, for each player, the number of played draws at each value of each stat, with the combined stats and milestones counted on their own because two stats are no longer paired by draw. Over, under and push shares, projections and the histograms read the same counts the live draws give; a test compares every market, side and line against a live simulation. A phone fetches about 26 KB a game instead of running 10,000 draws and writing about 10 MB of arrays to its database. The page checks the card hashes, seed, draw count and that every count adds up, and draws the game itself on any mismatch. A game with viewer lineup calls is always drawn in the browser. Browsers on different platforms can differ from Node in the last bit of a floating-point result, so a published draw and a browser's own draw of the same game are the same model and seed but not guaranteed bit for bit.

## Operations

`data/play-history.json.gz` is a build-only fixed-column/dictionary cache. It retains source IDs, sequence, clocks, players, scores and metadata; unchanged histories produce identical compressed bytes and are not rewritten. It is excluded from the public artifact. Team coverage denominators are authorized outcome IDs, not an invented complete league schedule; diagnostics include missing/undated events, retry deferrals, season coverage, reconciled FT games and fully reconstructed lineup games. Full input snapshots remain off unless ARCHIVE_INPUT_SNAPSHOTS=1.

`node --experimental-strip-types scripts/model-diagnostics.ts` writes component holdout results and their source hashes to `data/model-diagnostics.json`. This report is not a calibration certificate.

The refresh job's cron (minute 17 of 11, 14, 17, 20, 22, 23, 0, 1 and 2 UTC) is a backstop. GitHub dropped about two thirds of those slots and delayed the rest by up to hours, so every refresh, including a failed one, ends by arming `refresh-timer.yml`: a single waiting run (a newer timer cancels it) that dispatches the next refresh every 30 minutes within four hours of a tip, about 30 minutes before each tip, five minutes after Chicago midnight, and at least every two hours otherwise. Both the refresh's timer dispatch and the timer's refresh dispatch retry with backoff, because one GitHub 5xx used to end the chain. Timer runs pace like the schedule; a manual dispatch forces a full refresh. A Pages deployment that ends in failure is retried once under a new correlation id. Forecast archives keep only games tipping within two hours, which is where the evaluated last pre-tip card lives. Current roster/injury feeds are checked on every run; summary backfill runs in the four-hour pre-tip window or at day rollover, and is skipped outside those windows. The browser enforces a 90-minute source-age limit near tip. Its independent freshness workflow runs hourly: when no timer is waiting it starts a paced refresh to re-arm the chain, and after a missed three-hour deadline it requests a full recovery refresh and fails visibly. An open page polls the manifest every five minutes and reloads onto a newer generation once the viewer has been idle for two minutes or the tab is hidden. Both depend on GitHub scheduling; they cannot guarantee recovery from a GitHub-wide outage. Inspect the failed workflow notification, dispatch `refresh-rate-card.yml` on main, then verify `deploy-pages.yml` succeeds. A dispatch alone is not deployment success: the refresh job follows the correlated deployment run and fails on cancellation, failure or timeout. Re-running the refresh also retries deployment when data did not change.

Source checks run before data commits. If main changes during a build, publication fails instead of copying stale generated data onto new code. Scheduled automation uses the approved bot identity; approved manual execution uses Harsh4873. No production verification was performed for this maintenance pass.

The site uses system fonts. ESPN team/player images are optional third-party requests with no referrer; monograms remain available if loading fails.

Action references are pinned to full commit SHAs with Dependabot updates. CI uses Node 22.21.1, npm ci, an advisory check, DOM keyboard tests, semantic contrast checks and artifact/privacy validation. Calibration gates and later device/screen-reader review remain distinct from those automated checks.
