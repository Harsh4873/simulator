# Possessions v5 — 2026-11-06

This model uses ESPN rosters, injury designations and completed box scores. Box-score requests are restricted to event IDs in the permitted 2026/2027 public outcome history, at most 400 requested events per refresh. Recent completed boxes are revisited for corrections. The aggregate legacy shooting cache is not used for fitting; its population could not be audited by event. All production shooting and counting inputs now come from the same validated regular-season boxes.

This is an uncalibrated simulator. No sportsbook prices, spread captions or market totals enter fitting or sampling. Market comparisons are validation observations. A result near a posted line does not establish forecast accuracy.

## Data and publication

Required scoreboard, outcome, roster and injury failures stop publication. A malformed injury response is not an empty injury report. Explicitly empty reports are accepted. Completed boxes must contain both opponents, valid nonnegative integer counts, shooting identities, reconciled scores and plausible complete minutes. Partial/live/invalid boxes stay retryable. Regular-season fitting excludes preseason, postseason and play-in events. The 2026/2027 season restriction remains intentional until the permitted history lifecycle is expanded.

The slate and card have matching generation timestamps and SHA-256 checksums in a shared manifest. Both are validated before either public file is replaced; clients retry up to three coherent sets when generations or hashes differ. Unsupported model versions fail closed. A GitHub Pages artifact publishes both together. Publication requires usable active rosters, finite rates, per-team shooting exposure and unique athlete memberships. The browser rejects mismatched generations, wrong Chicago dates and source checks older than six hours (90 minutes within four hours of a tip), and rechecks after returning to the tab and every minute. These are operational expiry rules, not guarantees about injury-feed latency.

The off-site shooting cache stores completed event records so aggregates can be recomputed after corrections. `model_version`, `generator_sha`, `source_cutoff` and an input SHA-256 identify a card. Pre-tip cards and slates are archived under `data/forecast-archive/`; they and the shooting cache never enter `dist/`. Source bytes have a separate model hash. Identical substantive inputs and source hashes retain the card timestamp, bytes and seed. Successful source checks are recorded separately in ignored `.refresh-status.json` and passed to deployment as a matching health attestation; health is added only to the deployed artifact. Local rate-card builds require an explicit GENERATOR_SHA (a 40-character source commit or the deliberate scratch marker pending). Missing provenance and local-uncommitted are rejected, including when reusing an earlier generation. A pending card is explicitly uncommitted; it does not claim a committed source revision. Historical roster/injury snapshots were not retroactively invented.

## Minutes and participation

Long-term rate exposures weight the prior season 0.2 and current season 1. Current-role minutes are fitted separately: chronologically selected 5/10-appearance windows and optional 2/5-appearance half-lives blend with observed prior-season minutes (or the fitted population prior if absent). The prior strength is selected from 0/1/3/6/10 games by chronological minute-error comparison on preceding-season appearances. A separate final block measures minute errors and starter/bench residual dispersion. Fewer than 100 validation appearances retain a labelled three-game prior. Starts, team changes and evidence ages are recorded. Injury-return-specific effects remain unknown.

New summaries retain explicit participant/DNP records. Participation follows each team's explicit boxes: a player listed by a team (played or DNP) is tracked there until another team lists him, and a later explicit team box without him is an absence. Legacy boxes without explicit participation add nothing; omission there stays unknown. A rostered player never listed is absent for that team's explicit boxes this season; a player last listed elsewhere keeps that stint and is absent for the new team's later boxes. Features are role minutes over the last ten played games, played share of the last five team games, played last game, start share, absent share of the last five and the missed-game streak. The logistic fit uses Newton steps with ridge penalties 0.1/1/10/100 chosen on a chronological 60/20/20 event split, and is used only when the untouched block beats the base rate in both log loss and Brier. Training skips the first missed game after a played game: that is usually an injury or rest day that the pre-tip report already lists. Archived pre-tip reports then calibrate players listed healthy with a two-parameter logistic map on the model logit, fitted on the earlier 70% of archived games and used only when it improves the later 30% (at least 300 rows). Uncertain designations multiply that baseline by the archived share of same-designation players who played (at least 30 rows); otherwise labelled, uncalibrated multipliers apply: probable 0.9, doubtful 0.1, questionable/day-to-day 0.5. A designation-level rate never replaces a healthy player's own history. Out/suspended/inactive players receive zero. A player with no history at all keeps the Beta(1,1) scenario prior with known/unknown coverage counts. A rostered player with no NBA appearance in the permitted history starts from newcomer minutes: the mean of the first three appearances by players without a preceding-season appearance (a labelled ten-minute prior until 20 such appearances exist). Reported minute restrictions have a separate field and full-game cap; no restriction is inferred from an injury description. Historical injury-calibration fitting requires settled, pre-tip roster observations; without those records, status probabilities remain priors. Joint availability is sampled conditional on at least five players capable of full regulation and overtime minutes. This conditioning can raise marginal participation rates. The UI reports the actual simulated play share separately.

Positive minutes targets receive a mean-preserving gamma multiplier with the fitted residual SD (coefficient of variation capped at one) and proportional capped allocation to 240 team minutes; a truncated normal would add minutes to low-minute players before the budget is rescaled. There is no 24-minute cutoff or sequential bench residual assignment. Overtime happens only after a tie, uses the same participants and caps every player's increment at five, totaling 25 per team. Integer minute allocations are realized as one-minute stints with exactly five distinct players on each side. Every attempt, rebound, steal, block and assist is assigned only to a player in that stint. Observed starts and closing stints guide feasible synthetic rotations. Score/time strata can change starter/bench selection only if their fitted probabilities improve a later event block over the time-only baseline. Personal-foul hazards are estimated from box-reconciled play histories and fractional on-court exposure, including fouls at substitution boundaries. Frozen hazards generate Poisson increments at minute boundaries; conditioning on previous foul count is enabled only after improving held-out point-process loss. A sixth foul removes subsequent stints and overtime eligibility. If the sampled rotation is exhausted, a healthy coach-DNP with sufficient remaining minute capacity can enter; remaining available (including questionable) roster players and labelled replacement-level league-average lines can fill after that. Replacement-level lines copy observed rates from the card; they are not invented player stats. A team with no listed players, or with no observed shooting rates to copy, still fails visibly. Thin rotations stay playable and are flagged on the slate and box score. This is a minute-resolution approximation, not a positional depth chart or a validated joint foul/FT process.

## Possessions and production

The box-score possession estimator is `FGA + 0.44 FTA - ORB + TOV`. The 0.44 coefficient is the conventional approximation; FT trip sizes and types are estimated from numbered play sequences that reconcile with player box attempts. A game's expected pace averages the two fitted team paces; its standard deviation comes from historical regulation-equivalent pace. Each draw gives both teams the same integer possession budget. Overtime uses the same pace scaled to five minutes.

Team pace, turnovers, offensive rebounds and assists are shrunk toward the observed league population with a 12-game prior. Active players divide shot/FT opportunities by minutes times fitted attempt rates. Team target efficiency is the fitted team offense times a personnel factor (tonight's lineup implied PPP over the lineup the team actually played: each player's average minutes per team game this season, zeros included, so a season-long absence is not charged twice; older cards fall back to role minutes), multiplied by opponent defensive efficiency relative to the league and a venue term. The personnel factor is clamped to [0.7, 1.25]. Full-strength lineups keep the team rating; absences scale it. There is no geometric-mean shrinkage of team offense toward league-like player rates. Make probabilities are solved against this target before shots are sampled; scores are never adjusted after drawing. Offense, opponent defense, venue and log-rest differences are fitted together with ridge shrinkage. Earlier seasons get their own scoring-level shift, so the intercept is this season's league efficiency and also normalizes targets; a pooled intercept kept last season's higher scoring. Team, prior-season and level-shift strengths are selected by chronological holdout of predicted points (penalties 4/8/12, prior-season weights 0.2/0.5/1, level-shift penalties 50/200/800) and then refit on all events; venue/rest still use a 50-observation zero-effect prior. Neutral games omit the venue term. Rest is derived from Chicago calendar dates, including DST, with prior overtime workload. Travel distance and timezone changes require coordinates/timezones in permitted source responses and otherwise remain null. These are observational estimates, not causal effects.

Each possession can end in a turnover, field goal or ordinary FT trip. Numbered FT sequences fit two/three-shot shooting and bonus trip mixes, and-one probability, technical rate and retained-possession penalties. Unclassified trips stay labelled other. Personal take fouls are not automatically treated as retained-possession transition take fouls. Only field-goal misses and missed final ordinary/and-one free throws create rebound opportunities. Technical and retained-possession trips never create a rebound or consume an ordinary possession. A late leading-team FT adjustment (last two minutes, lead 1–8) is enabled only after improving held-out opportunity log loss. These fixed state boundaries are declared scenarios, not optimized thresholds or claims about a player's intent.

Offensive rebounds extend the possession. Offensive and defensive rebounds compete for the same opportunities; player shares use historical rebounding rates within the on-court five. Made field goals have at most one assister, excluding the scorer. Steals are a subset of opponent turnovers and blocks a subset of missed field goals. Team/dead-ball rebounds and the joint live bonus/foul/FT process remain approximations.

Lineup offense/defense uses observed substitution stints. Coefficients train on the first 60% of events, the next 20% selects the ridge penalty, and the final 20% evaluates it. Both stint and full-game baseline improvements and at least 20 completely reconstructed test games are required to enable it. The current fit fails this gate and is disabled. Creator-off-court usage also remains disabled when its untouched event block does not beat baseline; off-court associations are not causal injury effects.

Shot-mix and shooting priors use leave-athlete-out role pools and preceding-season chronological strength selection, with a separate final block for per-stat promotion. Shooting probability uncertainty uses beta posterior draws for accepted fits. Participation similarly separates training, penalty selection and final evaluation. Unknown historical roster and injury status remain explicit.

Frozen team-fit diagnostics report held-out score/efficiency covariance, margin/total SD and home/away/rest residual bias. These residuals include intrinsic possession noise, so they are not added wholesale as an extra browser shock. Predictive widths, status probabilities, and joint foul/FT outcomes remain uncalibrated. Older cards without matchup context use the fallback independent-stat sampler and should not be treated as the current model.

## Evaluation and UI

`npm run sanity` replays each current matchup with 10,000 draws and `fnv1a(event_id + ':' + generated_at)`. It reports a home spread with negative meaning home favored. It does not load market lines. Computation runs in a cancellable browser worker and is cached by event, substantive input hash, model hash and the viewer's lineup calls, with generation/version fallback for old cards. Lineups lets a viewer rule any rostered player in (plays every draw at role minutes, any reported restriction still applies) or out (zero minutes) for one game. Calls are stored only in that browser, applied to a copy of the card before sampling with the same seed, labelled on the score view, and never change published files. Worker progress is reported every 250 draws. Expected stat summaries are reused across scenario views. Forecast rows display all-draw expectations, including DNPs, so minutes still sum to the team budget. Player-panel expected lines average only draws with minutes greater than 0. Scenario rows display one draw. Prop probabilities condition on participation and exclude pushes; the panel explains the difference. Zero-minute DNP rows never enter shooting or counting-rate samples. Steals, blocks, turnovers and milestones are unavailable on older cards without those samples. P+A, P+R, R+A and double/triple-doubles preserve draw correlations. Milestones use the five standard categories at ten or more and settle yes as over 0.5; DNPs void. Total and home-margin distributions show means, medians, intervals, mean Monte Carlo SE and entered-line probabilities including pushes. Typicality uses mid-CDF and tied probability mass, evaluated at the actual displayed value.

`npm run replay` rebuilds each archived pre-tip card from its stored roster, injury report and training events with the current source, then scores 2,000 draws per game against settled boxes and the last archived pre-tip market quote (margin/total MAE and bias, win Brier, participation log loss, minutes and points errors). It is a diagnostic on a few dozen games, not a calibration certificate. `npm run backtest` writes `data/backtest-report.json`, using only event history from earlier Chicago dates. Same-date games are conservatively excluded because historical final timestamps are unavailable. It reports win Brier/log loss/reliability; margin/total CRPS, MAE, bias and interval coverage; and player-stat errors. A simple empirical historical baseline is scored on the same games, with paired game-cluster bootstrap intervals. Historical candidates are inferred from the preceding five team boxes, with unknown injuries. Ambiguous memberships are skipped and reported. Where a matching versioned, pre-tip archive with valid lineage exists, the harness replays it; otherwise the reconstruction is a diagnostic, not an archived forecast. Explicit DNP records support participation calibration where available; missing DNP/roster coverage remains unknown. Player errors cover matched actual participants, not a complete DNP/prop calibration population.

No confirmed historical closing-market archive is available. Permitted scoreboard quotes are now archived prospectively in `data/market-archive/`, independently of model inputs. Pre-tip paired moneylines support no-vig comparisons; an ESPN field named `close` is not treated as proof of an actual closing quote. Release gates require at least 500 untouched games and 2,000 settled player markets; absolute margin/total bias at most 1/2 points; 80%/90% coverage within three percentage points; ECE at most .03; and game-clustered evidence of improvement over baselines without material market-relative regression. Those gates have not passed. Prop policy remains uncalibrated. New cards publish per-stat exposure, age, prior fraction and empirical standard error; prices cannot override poor minute/role evidence. A versioned calibration artifact with as-of provenance and all release gates is required for promotion; a manually toggled boolean cannot certify a model.

The October 27 audit reports describe their named historical snapshots. Their parameters and sample forecasts are not the current specification.

## Operations

`data/play-history.json.gz` is a build-only fixed-column/dictionary cache. It retains source IDs, sequence, clocks, players, scores and metadata; unchanged histories produce identical compressed bytes and are not rewritten. It is excluded from the public artifact. Team coverage denominators are authorized outcome IDs, not an invented complete league schedule; diagnostics include missing/undated events, retry deferrals, season coverage, reconciled FT games and fully reconstructed lineup games. Full input snapshots remain off unless ARCHIVE_INPUT_SNAPSHOTS=1.

`node --experimental-strip-types scripts/model-diagnostics.ts` writes component holdout results and their source hashes to `data/model-diagnostics.json`. This report is not a calibration certificate.

The refresh job's cron (minute 17 of 11, 14, 17, 20, 22, 23, 0, 1 and 2 UTC) is a backstop. GitHub dropped about two thirds of those slots and delayed the rest by up to hours, so every refresh, including a failed one, ends by arming `refresh-timer.yml`: a single waiting run (a newer timer cancels it) that dispatches the next refresh every 45 minutes within four hours of a tip, about 30 minutes before each tip, five minutes after Chicago midnight, and at least every 3.5 hours otherwise. Timer runs pace like the schedule; a manual dispatch forces a full refresh. A Pages deployment that ends in failure is retried once under a new correlation id. Forecast archives keep only games tipping within two hours, which is where the evaluated last pre-tip card lives. Current roster/injury feeds are checked on every run; summary backfill runs in the four-hour pre-tip window or at day rollover, and is skipped outside those windows. The browser enforces a 90-minute source-age limit near tip. Its independent freshness workflow requests a recovery refresh and fails visibly after a missed deadline. Both depend on GitHub scheduling; they cannot guarantee recovery from a GitHub-wide outage. Inspect the failed workflow notification, dispatch `refresh-rate-card.yml` on main, then verify `deploy-pages.yml` succeeds. A dispatch alone is not deployment success: the refresh job follows the correlated deployment run and fails on cancellation, failure or timeout. Re-running the refresh also retries deployment when data did not change.

Source checks run before data commits. If main changes during a build, publication fails instead of copying stale generated data onto new code. Scheduled automation uses the approved bot identity; approved manual execution uses Harsh4873. No production verification was performed for this maintenance pass.

The site uses system fonts. ESPN team/player images are optional third-party requests with no referrer; monograms remain available if loading fails.

Action references are pinned to full commit SHAs with Dependabot updates. CI uses Node 22.21.1, npm ci, an advisory check, DOM keyboard tests, semantic contrast checks and artifact/privacy validation. Calibration gates and later device/screen-reader review remain distinct from those automated checks.
